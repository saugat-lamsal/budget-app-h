WORKED EXAMPLES, HER SETUP

Made-up numbers, worked out step by step, so you can check the money math
of her setup by hand. Every amount is in dollars and cents. Upstream's
examples are in worked-examples.txt, unchanged.

Each example is also a test (packages/money/test/worked-examples-h.test.ts).
The test reads the "Check:" line of every example straight from this file
and compares it with what the app works out. If a number here is wrong, or
the app disagrees with it, the test fails.

Her setup has no mom reserve and no old card (rulebook, "Off in this
setup"). Unless an example says otherwise, the settings are her starting
ones: split 50% savings / 30% needs / 20% wants, and "Ask before splitting
a paycheck" on, so a paycheck waits for her yes before it is split (3.1).

Rounding: each share is rounded DOWN to the cent. Any cents left over from
rounding go to savings (rule 3.3).

EXAMPLE 1. Her paycheck: nothing comes off first (3.3; 3.2 is off)
========================================================================

Before payday: every bucket is at $0.00.

Paycheck                                         2,345.67
It waits for her yes (3.1), and she says yes the day it arrives.

Step 1. No mom reserve in this setup: nothing comes off the top.
        Rest to split                            2,345.67

Step 2. Split it.
        Savings 50% of 2,345.67 = 1,172.835  ->  1,172.83
        Needs   30% of 2,345.67 =   703.701  ->    703.70
        Wants   20% of 2,345.67 =   469.134  ->    469.13
        Rounded shares add to 2,345.66, so 0.01 is left over.
        The leftover 0.01 goes to savings: 1,172.83 + 0.01 = 1,172.84

Step 3. Add it back up: 1,172.84 + 703.70 + 469.13 = 2,345.67
        That is the paycheck exactly.

Check: savings 1,172.84 | needs 703.70 | wants 469.13 | mom reserve 0.00

EXAMPLE 3. The app side of the self-check, with no mom reserve (12.2)
========================================================================

Setup day: savings $4,000.00, needs $1,000.00, wants $200.00, held for

EXAMPLE 4. A paycheck waiting for her yes (3.1, 51.1)
========================================================================

Setup day: savings $3,000.00, needs $400.00, wants $150.00. Nothing is
waiting. Her last paycheck was 14 days before Oct 9.

Oct 9: a paycheck of $1,987.65 posts. The paycheck rule matches it.

It waits in review. Nothing is split yet, so no bucket changes. It is
money in waiting in review (51.1): it counts on the app side of the
self-check, and not in safe to spend.

App side = savings + needs + wants + held for others
           + unsorted money in - unsorted spending
         = 3,000.00 + 400.00 + 150.00 + 0.00 + 1,987.65 - 0.00
         = 5,537.65
(The bank shows the $1,987.65 in checking too, so the two still match.)

Safe to spend = wants left - unsorted spending = 150.00 - 0.00 = 150.00

What Review shows it would get (3.3, with the split the next paycheck
uses):
        Savings 50% of 1,987.65 = 993.825  ->  993.82
        Needs   30% of 1,987.65 = 596.295  ->  596.29
        Wants   20% of 1,987.65 = 397.53   ->  397.53
        Rounded shares add to 1,987.64, so 0.01 goes to savings: 993.83

Check: savings 3,000.00 | needs 400.00 | wants 150.00 | unsorted money in 1,987.65 | app side 5,537.65 | safe to spend 150.00


========================================================================
EXAMPLE 5. She says yes three days later, after spending (3.1, B53)
========================================================================

The same as Example 4, then:

Oct 10: gas, $41.20, on the everyday card. She sorts it to needs.
        Needs: 400.00 - 41.20 = 358.80

Oct 12: she says yes to the paycheck.

Step 1. The new pay period starts on Oct 9, the day the paycheck
        arrived, not Oct 12 (B53). So the next payday is 14 days after
        Oct 9: Oct 23. On Oct 12 that is 11 days away.

Step 2. The leftover question for needs (3.4) asks about what needs held
        at her yes, before the paycheck: 358.80. (Wants: 150.00.)

Step 3. The paycheck is split as Example 4 showed:
        Savings 3,000.00 + 993.83 = 3,993.83
        Needs     358.80 + 596.29 =   955.09
        Wants     150.00 + 397.53 =   547.53
        The money waiting leaves review: unsorted money in 0.00.

Step 4. App side = 3,993.83 + 955.09 + 547.53 = 5,496.45
        That is Example 4's 5,537.65 less the gas, 41.20. No cent was
        made or lost.

(The test reads "days to the next payday" as 11.00: 11 days.)

Check: savings 3,993.83 | needs 955.09 | wants 547.53 | unsorted money in 0.00 | app side 5,496.45 | needs question 358.80 | days to the next payday 11.00

EXAMPLE 7. The bank side with both cards, pending charges included (12.1)
========================================================================

Her two cards are both used day to day (rulebook, section 1). In the app
they are the everyday card (Capital One) and the second card (Amex).

What the bank shows, posted:
        Checking                                  2,140.55
        Savings                                   6,300.00
        Everyday card owes                          412.18
        Second card owes                          1,075.92

Still pending:
        A debit card charge (checking)               15.00
        A charge on the everyday card                23.40
        A charge on the second card                  61.07

Step 1. Pending charges count (12.1): a debit lowers checking, a card
        charge raises what that card owes.
        Checking      2,140.55 - 15.00 = 2,125.55
        Everyday card   412.18 + 23.40 =   435.58
        Second card   1,075.92 + 61.07 = 1,136.99

Step 2. Bank side = checking + savings - everyday card - second card
                  = 2,125.55 + 6,300.00 - 435.58 - 1,136.99
                  = 8,425.55 - 435.58 - 1,136.99
                  = 7,989.97 - 1,136.99
                  = 6,852.98

Check: bank side 6,852.98

EXAMPLE 8. Setup day with both cards and a payment on its way (14.2, 12.5)
========================================================================

The bank as in Example 7. And yesterday 200.00 left checking to pay the
second card; it has not reached the card yet. Checking's 2,140.55 is
already without it; the second card still owes the 1,075.92.

Step 1. Starting money = bank side + money on its way (14.2, 12.5)
                       = 6,852.98 + 200.00
                       = 7,052.98

Step 2. She assigns it:
        Savings                                   6,300.00
        Needs                                       452.98
        Wants                                       300.00
        Held for others                               0.00
        Together: 6,300.00 + 452.98 + 300.00 = 7,052.98, all of it, so
        tracking starts (14.3).

Step 3. The self-check: bank side 6,852.98 + in transit 200.00
        = 7,052.98 = app side. Difference 0.00.

Step 4. Two days later the 200.00 lands. The second card owes
        1,136.99 - 200.00 = 936.99. Bank side = 2,125.55 + 6,300.00
        - 435.58 - 936.99 = 7,052.98, nothing in transit, and the
        difference is still 0.00. No bucket changed: paying a card is not
        spending (5.1).

Check: starting money 7,052.98 | in transit on setup day 200.00 | difference on setup day 0.00 | in transit after it lands 0.00 | difference after it lands 0.00 | needs 452.98

EXAMPLE 9. One payment to each card, the same amount, landing late (12.5)
========================================================================

Setup day: savings 1,000.00, needs 500.00, wants 250.00 (1,750.00 in
all). The bank: checking 1,400.00, savings 1,000.00, the everyday card
owes 300.00, the second card owes 350.00.
        1,400.00 + 1,000.00 - 300.00 - 350.00 = 1,750.00. It matches.

Oct 5: 300.00 leaves checking for the everyday card, and another 300.00
for the second card. Checking: 800.00. Neither has landed.
        In transit: 600.00.
        Bank side: 800.00 + 1,000.00 - 300.00 - 350.00 = 1,150.00
        Bank side + in transit = 1,750.00 = app side. Difference 0.00.

Oct 6: the second card's payment lands, a day late. The bank does not
say which payment it was. It is the same amount as both, but it can only
be the one sent to the second card (12.5: between the same accounts).
The second card owes 350.00 - 300.00 = 50.00.
        In transit: 300.00 (the everyday card's, still on its way).
        Bank side: 800.00 + 1,000.00 - 300.00 - 50.00 = 1,450.00
        1,450.00 + 300.00 = 1,750.00. Difference 0.00.

Oct 7: the everyday card's lands, two days late. It owes 0.00.
        In transit: 0.00. Bank side: 800.00 + 1,000.00 - 0.00 - 50.00
        = 1,750.00. Difference 0.00.

No bucket moved on any day: needs stays 500.00.

Check: in transit after both leave 600.00 | in transit after the second lands 300.00 | difference after the second lands 0.00 | in transit after both land 0.00 | difference after both land 0.00 | needs 500.00

EXAMPLE 10. The second card's statement, paid in parts (9.1, 9.3)
========================================================================

The second card's statement closed Oct 1: balance 812.40, minimum 35.00,
due Oct 26. Reminders come 7 and 2 days before: Oct 19 and Oct 24.

Oct 10: she pays the minimum, 35.00.
Oct 19: paid since the statement: 35.00. That is the minimum, but not the
        statement balance, so the reminder comes (9.3): one reminder.
Oct 20: she pays 777.39. Paid: 35.00 + 777.39 = 812.39, one cent short.
Oct 24: still one cent short of 812.40, so the reminder comes again.
        Still to pay: 812.40 - 812.39 = 0.01.
        That evening she pays the 0.01. Paid: 812.40, the full statement
        balance: no more reminders.

(The test reads the number of reminders as dollars: one reminder is 1.00,
none is 0.00.)

Check: paid by the first reminder 35.00 | reminders on the first reminder day 1.00 | paid by the second reminder 812.39 | reminders on the second reminder day 1.00 | still to pay on the second reminder day 0.01 | reminders after the last cent 0.00
